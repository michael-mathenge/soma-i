# SOMA.i video narration and cues

Maximum: five minutes. Aim below 4:45; use actual generated audio duration rather than an estimated speaking time. Narration is synthetic, not the owner's recorded voice.

## Scene 1: Problem and idea

SOMA.i means learning that leads somewhere. I developed the idea to explore a simple question: how can a learner connect what they study today to visible progress and a possible next step? My primary challenge track is Guidance, Pathways and Opportunity. Resource discovery and engagement support that journey. This is a working local proof of concept, not a production service. The narration in this demonstration is generated, and the learner data is fictional.

## Scene 2: Amina and pathway

Meet Amina Demo, our seeded Data Analyst learner. We assume she is a young adult in Nakuru using a basic phone and prepaid data. Those details are a design hypothesis, not research findings. Her pathway starts with Spreadsheets completed and SQL as the next checkpoint. The app also offers Frontend Developer and Backend Python Developer pathways. Learners can switch, skip or restart. The goal is to make the next action clear without locking someone into a single route.

## Scene 3: Reading and checkpoint

The recommended lesson cards show their source, related skills and a low data badge. This badge is a fixed content flag, not a measured bandwidth result. External resources retain source links, while seeded examples are labelled demo content. Here I open the SQL checkpoint and complete the three check-in questions. This is a demonstration with fictional data; selecting the practice attestation does not establish professional competence. The checkpoint turns a reading journey into a visible progress event.

## Scene 4: Result and opportunity

The result screen is the central moment. Amina has unlocked SQL. Her next checkpoint is Data Visualisation, and she sees relevant reading recommendations and a sample Junior Data Analyst opportunity. The opportunity is clearly labelled as a sample listing. Its match percentage compares the supplied skill with the example requirements; it is not a hiring probability or a measure of overall job readiness. Direct navigation to the result also has automated fallback coverage.

## Scene 5: Language and reminders

The interface can switch between English and Kiswahili. This does not translate every external lesson or every pathway term. Reminder preferences are optional, and a phone number is not required. In the fresh rehearsal, the console reminder printed once, then a second attempt printed zero reminders. A separate reminder-alert command reported one alert and suppressed its repeat. No production SMS or email was sent. The historical local schedule is documented as paused.

## Scene 6: AI development and evidence

I originated the SOMA.i idea. Claude helped with planning and review notes, and Codex helped me implement, test, debug and document the system. My biggest lesson was getting started with AI development and engineering: moving from my own concept to a working model. Recorded validation includes one hundred and seventeen backend tests, sixteen database-guard passes with one skip, fourteen robustness tests, lint, a production build, and sixteen Playwright tests. These checks support the tested scenarios, not production readiness. Database-safety mistakes and an interrupted validation attempt are disclosed in the repository.

## Scene 7: Limits and next steps

SOMA.i connects a lesson, a checkpoint, an unlocked skill and a possible next step. The remaining work is to validate the problem with real learners, assess whether recommendations are useful, and review language and accessibility with users. Offline reload has not been demonstrated, and progress submissions require connectivity. Accounts, real SMS, live vacancy aggregation and deployment remain outside scope. The repository includes setup instructions, the reflection, AI-use disclosure, skill scan and documented evidence. Thank you for reviewing this proof of concept.
